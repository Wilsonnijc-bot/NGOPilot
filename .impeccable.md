## Design Context

### Users

NGOPilot serves frontline NGO staff, especially Hong Kong community-care workers, social workers, and roster owners. They use a local AI agent to turn forms, recordings, templates, and scheduling inputs into reviewable operational outputs while keeping sensitive data, provenance, and human approval visible.

### Brand Personality

Careful, practical, and quietly competent. The interface should feel orderly, trustworthy, human-supervised, and respectful of sensitive case work. AI is a drafting assistant, never the final authority.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the shared "archival care" visual language established by the CareFlow and RosterCopiilot frontends: warm paper surfaces, dark ink text, cinnabar actions, sage success states, and amber review or warning states. The product should resemble an organized case-work desk or archive rather than generic health-tech, corporate SaaS, or a futuristic AI wrapper. Avoid bright hospital blue, purple-blue gradients, glassmorphism, neon accents, and decorative marketing imagery.

For color-normalization work, use the dependency apps' implemented palette as the source of truth:

- Paper 100 `#f8f3e8`
- Paper 150 `#f3ecdd`
- Paper 200 `#efe7d3`
- Paper 300 `#dccfb0`
- Ink 400 `#5a544a`

- Ink 900 `#0e0c0a`

- Amber Ink 500 `#a8841f`

### Design Principles

1. Keep the interface document-first and operational; forms, evidence, review state, and outputs carry the visual hierarchy.
2. Use paper and ink for the default environment, reserving cinnabar for actions and risk, sage for confirmed states, and amber for pending or caution states.
3. Build trust through inspectability: show source, status, provenance, uncertainty, and required human confirmation clearly.
4. Preserve dense-workflow legibility with strong contrast and status labels that do not rely on color alone.
5. Prefer restrained, functional detail over decorative UI, marketing patterns, or theatrical AI presentation.
