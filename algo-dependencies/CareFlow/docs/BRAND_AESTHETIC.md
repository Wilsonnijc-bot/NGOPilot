# CareFlow Aesthetic Brand Statement

Generated from the current CareFlow interface and product direction on 2026-06-29.

## Brand Position

CareFlow is an AI administrative assistant for community care teams. Its aesthetic should make technology feel accountable, reviewable, and close to real social-service paperwork. The brand is not a generic health-tech SaaS look. It is a calm, document-first workspace for people who handle forms, visits, welfare applications, and sensitive elder-care records every day.

The visual system should feel like:

- A social worker's organized desk.
- A case archive with clear audit trails.
- A stamped and reviewed document workflow.
- A quiet editorial tool rather than a promotional website.
- Human supervision first, AI assistance second.

The core promise is simple: CareFlow gives paperwork time back to care workers without hiding judgment, privacy, or accountability behind automation.

## Aesthetic Thesis

CareFlow's brand language is "archival care." It combines paper texture, ink, red review stamps, serif editorial headings, compact folio labels, and practical workflow tables. The interface should feel tactile without becoming decorative, warm without becoming sentimental, and technical without becoming cold.

The current product already expresses this through:

- Warm paper backgrounds instead of white dashboards.
- Ink-black text and rules instead of blue corporate chrome.
- Cinnabar red actions and stamps instead of generic primary buttons.
- Serif titles and case-file language.
- Monospace folio labels for IDs, dates, model names, and system state.
- Explicit review, export, burn, and audit states.

## Personality

CareFlow should sound and look:

- Careful.
- Practical.
- Quietly competent.
- Clerical in the best sense: orderly, exact, and trustworthy.
- Respectful of sensitive information.
- Aware that AI output is draft work until a human reviews it.

It should not feel:

- Magical.
- Futuristic for its own sake.
- Overly playful.
- Medical-corporate.
- Crypto/dashboard-like.
- Marketing-heavy.
- Like a generic AI wrapper.

## Visual Identity

### Name and Mark

The English product name is `CareFlow`. The current interface also uses the Chinese mark `護流`, supported by the square stamp-like symbol `護`.

The mark should be treated as a document stamp rather than a glossy logo. It works best as a compact red or ink-toned seal, slightly imperfect, paired with a restrained wordmark.

Recommended usage:

- Use `CareFlow` in English-facing documents.
- Use `護流` when presenting the Hong Kong community-care context.
- Use the stamp mark as a small identity anchor, not as a large decorative emblem.
- Keep the mark close to workflow, review, filing, and approval contexts.

### Color Palette

The palette is warm, restrained, and document-native.

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Background | Paper 50 | `#fcfaf3` | Main app background |
| Surface | Paper 100 | `#f8f3e8` | Sidebars, cards, sheets |
| Divider | Paper 200 | `#efe7d3` | Soft section boundaries |
| Strong divider | Paper 300 | `#dccfb0` | Table lines, secondary borders |
| Muted text | Ink 400 | `#5a544a` | Secondary text and helper copy |
| Body text | Ink 700 | `#1f1c18` | Main readable text |
| Deep text | Ink 900 | `#0e0c0a` | Headings, rules, strong labels |
| Primary action | Cinnabar 500 | `#a8412c` | Review stamps, primary action, focus accents |
| Danger/action emphasis | Cinnabar 700 | `#6b2419` | Destructive or high-risk action |
| Success | Sage 500 | `#4a6c5d` | Reviewed, live, confirmed |
| Warning | Amber Ink 500 | `#a8841f` | Pending, mock, low-confidence, incomplete |

Color rules:

- Use paper and ink as the default visual environment.
- Use cinnabar only when the user should notice action, review, risk, or identity.
- Use sage for completed, live, confirmed, or healthy states.
- Use amber for pending, inferred, mock, or cautionary states.
- Avoid bright hospital blue, neon AI gradients, glassmorphism, and large purple-blue gradient surfaces.

## Typography

CareFlow uses an editorial hierarchy:

- Serif for titles, section names, and document-like headings.
- Sans for body text and operational labels.
- Monospace for folios, IDs, dates, model names, counters, and technical details.

Current font direction:

- Serif: `Noto Serif TC`, `Songti TC`, `Songti SC`, `STSong`, serif.
- Sans: `PingFang HK`, `PingFang TC`, `Helvetica Neue`, `Hiragino Sans GB`, `Microsoft JhengHei`, system UI.
- Mono: `JetBrains Mono`, `SF Mono`, `Menlo`, monospace.

Typography should be compact and legible. Large serif headings are appropriate for page-level titles, but dense workflow screens should prioritize scannability over drama.

## Layout Principles

CareFlow layouts should resemble a case desk, not a landing page.

Use:

- Left navigation as an archive shelf.
- Thin rules and table borders to structure information.
- Case numbers, folio labels, and pipeline symbols for orientation.
- Dense but breathable spacing.
- Three-column review layouts when comparing source evidence, extracted fields, and actions.
- Fixed workflow steps and explicit status labels.

Avoid:

- Oversized marketing hero sections inside the product.
- Decorative nested cards.
- Large empty panels that hide operational information.
- Rounded pill-heavy UI.
- Abstract AI illustrations.
- One-note color washes.

## Component Language

### Stamps

Status badges should look like document stamps. They are rectangular, low-radius, uppercase or compact, and tied to workflow state.

Examples:

- `pending_review`: amber stamp.
- `confirmed`: sage stamp.
- `failed`: cinnabar stamp.
- `mock`: amber stamp.
- `live`: sage stamp.
- `AI inferred`: amber stamp.

### Buttons

Primary actions should feel like committing a document action, not clicking a generic web CTA.

Use:

- Dark ink buttons for primary navigation or neutral confirmation.
- Cinnabar stamp buttons for review, submit, export, render, or approve actions.
- Ghost buttons for secondary actions.
- Clear destructive styling for burn/delete actions.

Button labels should be specific: `Export`, `Render DOCX`, `Start Review`, `Burn Transcript`, `Run Diagnostics`.

### Forms

Inputs should feel like paper fields:

- Underline-style text inputs for ordinary fields.
- Thin bordered text areas or selects when a larger writing surface is needed.
- Minimal radius.
- Clear focus through cinnabar or ink.
- Labels using folio or numbered field language when appropriate.

### Tables

Tables are a core CareFlow surface. They should be calm, archival, and easy to scan.

Use:

- Serif or compact uppercase headers.
- Thin horizontal rules.
- Monospace counters and dates.
- Muted secondary metadata.
- Status stamps aligned consistently.

### Upload Areas

Upload zones should feel like "receiving documents" rather than cloud-storage widgets.

Use:

- Dashed ink borders.
- Paper surfaces.
- Cinnabar drag state.
- Copy that references receiving, filing, templates, recordings, or case creation.

## Copywriting Voice

CareFlow's writing should be direct, operational, and accountable.

Preferred tone:

- "AI extracted this field. A reviewer must confirm it before export."
- "Transcript is encrypted at rest and can be burned after review."
- "This batch has unreviewed records. Export anyway?"
- "Mock mode is active because this provider has no API key."

Avoid:

- "Let AI handle everything."
- "Magic document automation."
- "Revolutionary care intelligence."
- "One-click perfect records."
- "No human work required."

The product can be warm, but warmth should come from respect for care workers' time and the seriousness of their work, not from soft marketing language.

## AI Presentation

AI should be framed as a drafting and extraction layer, never as the final authority.

Every AI surface should make these ideas visible:

- AI output is provisional.
- Human review is required before export or rendering.
- Confidence, source, missing fields, and inferred values should be shown.
- Mock/live provider state should be visible in diagnostic or system contexts.
- Sensitive data handling should be explicit, especially for transcripts.

CareFlow should never visually imply that automation removes responsibility. The brand depends on the opposite: automation is useful because it remains inspectable.

## Privacy and Trust Aesthetic

Privacy should appear as part of the product's operating ritual, not as legal boilerplate.

Visual cues:

- Red side rules for sensitive warnings.
- Stamps for reviewed, burned, failed, and pending states.
- Monospace file/session identifiers.
- Clear status history and timestamps.
- Plain explanations for encryption, local storage, mock mode, and transcript burning.

Trust should be built through evidence, not reassurance. Show what happened, what model/provider was used, what remains unreviewed, and where the output file came from.

## Imagery and Illustration

CareFlow should use real workflow artifacts when images are needed:

- Forms.
- Scanned paper.
- PDF pages.
- DOCX templates.
- Case tables.
- Annotated bounding boxes.
- Review panels.

Avoid generic healthcare stock photos, smiling clinicians, abstract AI brains, neon networks, and decorative vector scenes. The interface itself should be the primary visual asset.

## Motion and Interaction

Motion should be minimal and functional.

Use:

- Subtle hover states.
- Small transitions on borders, stamps, and active nav items.
- Polling or extraction indicators that communicate waiting without theatrics.

Avoid:

- Large animated backgrounds.
- Confetti.
- Excessive loading spinners.
- AI "thinking" theatrics that distract from review accountability.

## Accessibility and Legibility

CareFlow handles dense administrative work, so legibility is a brand requirement.

Rules:

- Maintain strong contrast between ink text and paper surfaces.
- Do not rely on color alone for status; pair color with labels.
- Keep small folio text secondary and never as the only critical instruction.
- Make review actions and destructive actions visually distinct.
- Ensure dense tables remain readable on common laptop screens.

## Do and Do Not

Do:

- Keep the interface document-first.
- Use paper, ink, cinnabar, sage, and amber consistently.
- Show review state clearly.
- Make file, provider, and export provenance visible.
- Treat AI as a draft assistant.
- Preserve a Hong Kong community-care context when relevant.

Do not:

- Rebrand CareFlow as a generic AI automation platform.
- Use glossy gradients, floating blobs, or futuristic imagery.
- Hide uncertainty, mock mode, or missing fields.
- Make marketing pages feel more important than operational workflows.
- Add decorative UI that competes with forms, files, and review evidence.

## One-Sentence Brand Statement

CareFlow is a paper-warm, stamp-driven AI care administration desk: built to turn forms, recordings, and templates into reviewed outputs while keeping human judgment, privacy, and evidence visible at every step.

## Practical Design North Star

When adding a new CareFlow screen, ask:

1. Does this feel like a careful case-work desk rather than a generic SaaS page?
2. Can the user see what is AI-generated, what is human-reviewed, and what is still pending?
3. Are sensitive records treated with visible restraint?
4. Does the page help a care worker finish paperwork faster without hiding responsibility?

If the answer is yes, the design is on brand.
